# NOOP v10.5.0

A release about seeing your training as a shape over time, getting numbers that used to need a tape
measure, and — most of all — your phone doing far less work to tell you the same things.

## Training load

**CTL, ATL and form (#1423, #1425).** NOOP now models chronic and acute training load and the balance
between them, and draws all three on a Trends card. Fitness accumulates slowly, fatigue quickly, and the
gap between them is what tells you whether a hard block is building you up or digging a hole. Computed
on-device from the strain you already have; no new data is collected.

**Sleep-versus-wake heart rate (#1424).** The contrast between your sleeping and waking heart rate,
surfaced as its own signal rather than buried inside recovery.

## A VO₂max you can actually get

**Estimated without a waist measurement (#1391, #1406, #1393).** The previous estimate needed a waist
circumference many people never enter. If NOOP knows your resting heart rate, it can now use the Uth
heart-rate-ratio method from age and sex alone, and it names which method produced the figure. An
estimate that says how it was reached is worth more than one that doesn't.

## Battery

**Stop re-scoring days that haven't changed (#1005, #1395, #1396, #1402).** NOOP re-analysed a rolling
window every fifteen minutes, including days whose data had not moved since the last pass. A per-day
reuse cache now skips that work, on both WHOOP 4.0 and 5.0/MG. This was the largest background drain on
Android and it was reported, measured and chased down with logs by **@bartmuskala**.

**Low refresh, and Power saving where you can find it (#1430, #1431).** A sub-option syncs hourly at any
charge instead of every fifteen minutes — fewer reconnections is the biggest saving on a WHOOP 4.0 — and
Power saving moved into More, with the battery threshold now reaching 35%.

**Fewer diagnostics running forever.** Shadow instrumentation from the R-R over-count investigation was
doing six sorts of a night's beats per night, on every affected strap, four times an hour. Two
measurements that had finished their job were retired, and what replaced them costs one pass.

## Apple Health, and your ring

**Automatic Apple Health write-back (#1432, thanks @MikaSchultes).** Workouts can flow back into Apple
Health without a manual export.

**Hourly step import (#1429).** iPhone steps arrive hour by hour, with a 90-day backfill, instead of one
lump per day.

**More of the Oura ring decoded, and read honestly (thanks @pipiche38).** The ring's own breath rate is
decoded and shown as instrumentation rather than scored (#1384, #1450); its step features are decoded as
Tier-B instrumentation (#1359); the skin-temperature "worn" gate now allows for a ring whose heart-rate
and temperature sensors run on independent clocks, which was blanking the card entirely (#1467); and the
Rhythm view says "no data" outright on hardware that cannot produce the reading, instead of showing an
empty chart (#1360).

## Honesty and accuracy

A large part of this release is numbers that were quietly wrong, or quietly guessed:

- **A completed sync no longer reports itself as interrupted** (#1466). A WHOOP 4.0 routinely ends a
  full night's offload on an idle timeout; NOOP called that "sync interrupted" even when the whole night
  had landed.
- **Diagnostic exports report the strap that actually connected** (#1451, #1464). Two logs from WHOOP 5
  straps were headed "WHOOP 4.0", and on Apple the header had never reported a model at all.
- **Battery percent refreshes twice as fast while charging**, because that is the one time the number
  moves while you are watching it.
- **The strap reconnect survives suspension** (#1421), and a 5.0/MG that does serve history no longer
  thrashes its link every two minutes (#1414, thanks @justinjor-bit).
- **Re-scoring no longer silently skips** Oura, watch and re-added straps (#1392, thanks @pipiche38).

## Cross-platform correctness

**@bhelm** contributed roughly thirty fixes in this release, almost all of the same kind: places where
the Swift and Kotlin implementations of one calculation disagreed. Half-tie rounding in recovery traces,
FIT coordinates and connection durations; signed zero surviving a clamp; DST-spanning stress windows;
sleep-stage clipping; malformed UTF-8 in the Oura text decoders; JSON control characters in Android
backups; Unicode normalization of custom lab-marker keys. Individually small, collectively the reason
the two platforms produce the same numbers from the same bytes.

**@vishk23** extended the golden decoder oracle past frame decode (#869) — and it immediately found four
places where a 32-bit narrowing on Kotlin would have silently discarded all history from 2038, or today
on a strap with a future-dated clock.

**@terry-zeng1134** fixed the AltStore manifest's declared permissions (#1388), and **@Duzafizzl** built
out the Breathe protocol catalog with holds, session lengths and translations (#1390).
