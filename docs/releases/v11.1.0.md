# NOOP v11.1.0

A release about NOOP saying what is true, and letting you choose how you read it.

## Choose your clock

Times were decided by your device's **region**, not by you. On Apple that is deliberate — it keeps your
region's date order and clock style — but building a locale from a region identifier throws away the
explicit **24-Hour Time** switch in iOS Settings. Someone in a 24-hour country who preferred a 12-hour
clock had no way to get one.

Settings → Appearance now has **Clock: System / 12-hour / 24-hour**. It defaults to System, so no
existing display changes unless you ask, and System now follows your phone's own switch.

It reaches the times you actually read: the Asleep/Woke row, workout and nap times, caffeine and
hydration entries, backup timestamps, sleep-stage timelines, and the home-screen widgets. Chart axis
ticks deliberately stay 24-hour for now — 12-hour labels are wider and the axis spacing needs looking at
on a real device before that changes.

## Sleep without motion

Sleep detection is built on stillness, so a strap that banks no movement produced no nights at all. That
is the everyday reality of a WHOOP 5/MG that will not complete pairing: live heart rate streams fine and
nothing is ever recorded.

NOOP can now find and stage a night from heart rate alone. It is **display-only on purpose**: an HR-only
night never contributes to your resting heart rate, HRV or SDNN baselines. Those describe your
physiology, and a night detected without the usual evidence has not earned a place in them.

## Logs that report instead of asserting

Two diagnostics landed because two different reports could not be answered from a log.

Every disconnect now writes one line saying how long the link held, how many frames and bytes arrived,
whether the realtime stream was armed, and how it ended. Previously a log could not state whether a strap
had transmitted anything — you had to infer it from the fact that every logged line happened to be
outgoing, which measures NOOP's logging rather than the strap.

The clock exchange now quotes the strap's own reply. NOOP wrote "clock synced — strap can persist history
now" the instant it queued the write, before any answer existed, so a log could assert the clock was set
while the connection readout said 1970/71 — two contradictory statements with nothing to tell them apart.
It now says what it **sent**, and logs the reply frame verbatim. On a 5/MG the readout also said "RTC
reads 1970/71" when no clock had ever been read on that family; it now reports what it actually has,
which is how the strap dated its records.

## Everything else

- The 1970/71 warning takes battery into account, so an already-charged strap is not told to charge (#1818).
- The AI coach receives deep, REM and light minutes plus sleep efficiency (#1816).
- Export warns when a backup exceeds the 2 GiB restore ceiling, and an oversize restore can be allowed
  through instead of refused (#1807).
- Widget heart-rate and HRV values match their icons again, and both platforms' widgets read aloud
  properly (#1795, thanks @Sneheth; #1799).
- The live-workout timer no longer sits under the pause button (#1814), and the steps calibration card
  stops asking for data you have already provided (#1815).
- Oura rings that advertise no name label consistently across platforms (#1797, thanks @pipiche38), and
  Android's standard heart-rate logging is honest about how much it captured (#1796, thanks @kvnloo).

## Still open

WHOOP 5/MG history remains the hard problem. A strap that will not complete the pairing handshake is
never given a clock, and an un-clocked 5/MG does not persist sensor data to flash — so its offloads
complete with nothing in them. The diagnostics above exist to tell that case apart from a strap that was
clocked and still refuses, which is a different fault reported on the same firmware.
