# NOOP v11.7.0

A release about the app keeping up with you, and about readings meaning what they say.

## A stress screen that keeps up

Scoring a day of stress reads hundreds of thousands of samples. That work was happening on the same
thread that draws the screen, so Today and Stress could sit unresponsive while they loaded. It now
runs off that thread on both platforms, the baseline is reduced a day at a time rather than held a
month at once, and the unprompted rescore yields to whatever you are doing instead of competing with
it for the machine.

The home-screen widget fills on its own schedule without the app being opened. A rescore that produces
nothing is retried rather than being allowed to spend the whole refresh interval, and the Today curve
is kept as fresh as the screen it links to.

The readings themselves now agree with each other. The 0 to 3 level has one spelling across the widget
and the card, and is no longer rounded a second time on its way to the widget. The screen draws its
line from the same sliding read the number is taken from. Movement is drawn as the stretches it
actually covers rather than as dots on the axis. An early morning says the day has not started rather
than reporting Calibrating, which reads like something is wrong.

## WHOOP 5 readings in the units the strap sends

Thanks to @Trillient, two decoding faults are corrected.

R-R intervals arriving on the standard heart-rate profile were converted as though they were in the
Bluetooth spec's units. A WHOOP 5 sends milliseconds directly, so every interval taken from that
profile came out 2.3% low, and HRV computed from it inherited the error. What settled it was a paired
capture: the same beats arriving on both the strap's own channel and the standard one carry
byte-identical values, so only one of the two conversions could be right.

The console log's record header was read as a 16-bit counter spanning two bytes. Only the first is a
counter, and it wraps at 255. Across 2,978 records from a single night the second byte never moved
through nine of those wraps, which a real counter could not do. Both platforms now decode the two
fields separately.

## An Oura ring that stops repeating itself

Thanks to @pipiche38 for a sustained run of corrections to the ring's history path.

A replayed burst that the ring's own clock disproves as a reboot no longer resets the history resume
cursor, and no longer announces a reboot that did not happen. A night that has just been offloaded is
scored immediately rather than waiting for the next periodic tick. History is fetched every five
minutes rather than every fifteen, the anchor check judges a ten second gap rather than only a thirty
second one, and an onset rekey is refused when the assembler's own clip never bound.

There is also a feature-mode control and a consolidated set of Oura diagnostics in the Test Centre,
the raw night sidecar stays in the export, and the SpO2 sample keeps only the channel that carries a
reading.

## Scores that survive a re-score

Thanks to @bhelm. WHOOP 5 HRV and Recovery, and the vitals derived from R-R, were being dropped by a
re-score pass that could not recompute them, so a value you already had could disappear on a pass that
had nothing better to put in its place. Both are now preserved.

## Readings that say when they are at a limit

A Fitness Age resting at the end of its scale now says so, rather than presenting a bound as though it
were a measurement, and points at the number that still moves when the age stops. On Sleep, a night
already detected and staged no longer sits under a banner claiming it is still being calculated, and
browsing back to a night with no stage data no longer shows the most recent night's date beneath it.

## Diagnostics that name the cause

A device reporting no R-R now says whether the beats were never banked or were refused by the unit
policy, which are different problems with different answers. The nightly HRV summary says why it
reported nothing. A retired probe tells whoever reads its output which switch to turn on, and stops
under-reporting its own retirement. A re-arm clears a refusal latch that previously could not be
reached once set. The sync chip surfaces the strap's backlog.
