# NOOP v11.0.0

A major release, and the version number is about one thing above all: the WHOOP 5.0 and MG finally hold
a connection. Everything else — a body clock on the Sleep screen, nights that read straighter, a Journal
that stops counting silence against you — arrived alongside it.

## The WHOOP 5.0 / MG connection

**A handshake the strap never answers no longer takes the link with it (#1635, #1642, thanks
@Zebsi235).** NOOP wrote a CLIENT_HELLO on every connect and the 5/MG never acknowledged it; roughly
4.8 seconds later the link dropped, and the cycle repeated all day. NOOP now recognises the pattern,
stops writing the handshake for that strap, and keeps the link — the "Live HR, not fully paired" state
the app already modelled, reached deliberately instead of never. A field capture confirmed a link held
for over seven hours where it previously lasted five seconds.

An HCI capture contributed by @Zebsi235 settled what the strap is actually doing: it answers the
pairing request with `Pairing Not Supported`. That is a refusal, not a timing problem, and it ended
weeks of guessing.

**Reconnecting costs what it should (#1778, #1779, #1781).** Tapping Connect used to throw away
everything NOOP had learned and re-earn it over five more dropped links. It now keeps that knowledge,
gives the handshake exactly one fresh attempt, and settles after a single reconnect. A timer firing is
no longer treated as you asking to try again.

**The strap is asked properly, and says so in the log (#1639, #1646, #1648, #1652, #1653, #1656,
#1668).** NOOP now observes the OS pairing flow rather than inferring it, reads the Device Information
Service on a strap that never bonds, enumerates what a strap actually offers once per device, and lets
a 5/MG identify itself instead of being guessed at from its serial.

**Clearing a stale phone pairing (#1790).** When the phone holds a pairing the strap no longer honours,
NOOP can now clear it for you — off by default, in Test Centre, once per streak, and only after the
guide has already asked you to do it by hand.

## Sleep

**Your body clock (#1722, #1723, #1726, #1729, #1731, #1733).** A 24-hour dial on the Sleep screen
showing your circadian phase, with an absolute chronotype bucketed from it. The diagnostics report the
inputs it used, and a rhythm too small to be real is refused rather than drawn.

**A partial night is not a whole one (#1717, #1721, #1734).** A hypnogram that only partly downloaded
was scored as though complete, which quietly shortened nights after an interrupted sync. Both readers
of that coverage now agree on what "complete" means.

**Fragmented nights are stitched back together (#1755, #1782).** When data is sparse, NOOP bridges
short gaps between sleep runs — and now allows a longer interruption when your heart rate stayed in the
sleep band throughout, because on a strap with no motion data heart rate is the better witness.

**Sleep debt you can act on (#1741).** Debt is now expressed as a target for tonight rather than an
abstract ledger.

## Journal, workouts and metrics

**Yes days are compared against No days (#322).** Previously an insight weighed the days you logged
against every day you did not — so a week you never opened the app counted as a week of No.

**Pause and resume a live workout (#1690).** The controls are on the active-workout card, and the five
clocks that used to ignore a pause now honour it.

**Latest Workouts opens a session (#1701, #1703).** Tapping a row goes to its detail, and iOS uses the
same 14-day window Android already did.

**Skin temperature leads with the absolute (#1663, #1665, #1671, #1708).** The nightly absolute is
stored and shown first, deviation beneath it, in your own units throughout.

**VO₂max says why a line breaks (#1740).** The chart shows the decimal it draws, and explains a gap
rather than leaving one.

## Straps, sync and privacy

**Multiple straps are kept apart (#1455, #1634, #1730, #1745).** Physiology is read across every
registered strap, firmware is attributed to the strap it came from rather than the last to connect,
last-sync belongs to a strap rather than the install, and a 5/MG carries a stable identity derived from
its serial.

**Health Connect permissions by category (#1509, thanks @kavemang).** Recovery and wellness, Activity,
and Body composition are picked before Android's prompt. Existing installs are detected and keep every
category they already had.

**A crash you can report (#1718, #1720, #1724).** Android shows a copyable recovery screen after a
crash, redacted everywhere it leaves the device — and an upgrade no longer greets you with a crash from
the build it replaced.

**Update checking, on by default (#1674, #1675).** Both platforms notice a new release. The privacy
documentation now describes what actually happens.

**Experimental self-hosted push (#1631, #1700).** One-way push to your own endpoint, off by default,
with the destination errors localized.

## Oura

**A bonded ring with no advertised name is listed again (#1776, thanks @pipiche38).** A bonded ring
often stops advertising a name, and that was exactly the ring the Add-Device screen could not show.

**Skin Temp as a Key Metrics tile (#1678), and protocol corrections (#1629, #1687, #1777).**

## Under the hood

Raw 100 Hz IMU capture and labeled recording sessions for the 5/MG (#1709). A JSON query CLI for the
local read tools (#1752). Analysis reads each row once instead of 2.25 times (#1695, #1696). Swift
analytics tests run on Linux (#1529). Several diagnostics now say which gate refused a metric, rather
than leaving a blank.
